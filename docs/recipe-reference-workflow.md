# Recipe Intelligence: approved writing and reference-photo workflow

Open **Recipe Intelligence** from the desktop shortcut or Windows launcher. The URL workspace opens first.

1. Paste a recipe URL and click **Get estimate**. Reading the recipe and downloading its photographs are free. All usable source photographs are selected automatically, including when there are more than three.
2. Review the full estimate and image count. The spending limit is prefilled from the quoted reservation and can be changed under **Adjust spending limit**.
3. Click **Generate article + images**. The saved article and edited images appear in **My articles**. Downloads, preview and WordPress copy remain available.

**Article and image options** contains optional model, color, output and automation controls. **Uploaded photos and saved preparations** retains manual photo editing and uploads. Changed selections still require an updated estimate before generating.

An unfinished or blocked source never appears as a free, runnable job. If a website refuses automatic access, the app shows the source error and offers **Website blocked? Paste the recipe instead**. Keep the source URL and paste its title, an Ingredients heading and an Instructions heading with their contents. This creates a new preparation without fetching the blocked page. It retains the supplied facts and does not invent missing times or servings. If no source photographs are available, the quote explicitly includes three new text-based recipe images. Nothing is charged until Generate is clicked.

Repeated estimate requests for the same preparation and settings reuse the unpaid draft. Existing paid jobs retain their settings, references, instructions and receipts. Full automatic remains the default; optional review modes are available under the options disclosure. Missing essential facts, uncertain provider outcomes and actual technical failures remain visible.

## Instructions and snapshots

The user's complete Premium Master Prompt V4, followed by mandatory section 18 on source privacy and editorial independence, lives in `lib/recipe-intelligence/masterRecipePrompt.ts` (`recipe-master-v4`). New articles use the compatible `master_recipe_v1` format key. The runtime adapter and structured fields live in `prompts.ts`; the renderer lives in `article-document.ts`. V4 groups ingredient explanations naturally, removes repeated advice and leaves unhelpful sections empty. Its separate `articleTitle` keeps source branding out of headings and recipe-card metadata. Publishable HTML, Markdown, text and recipe-card JSON no longer append a source footer, `isBasedOn` or the authoritative research object. Provenance remains in private local `source.json`, `research-sources.json`, editorial notes and frozen job records. Full download bundles can contain these editorial records and are not intended to be published wholesale. Previously saved articles and frozen requests are retained. The previous fixed-length blog and compact profiles remain available for historical jobs. Canonical photo-editing and high-detail understanding instructions live in `photoProfile.ts`. The two project skills in `.agents/skills/` point to these definitions and the saved examples in `tests/fixtures/approved-recipe-photos/`.

The master article prioritizes accuracy and useful natural American English over length. It requests an approximately 80–130-word introduction, ingredient explanations, numbered method, useful tips, serving/storage/variation guidance, meaningful optional FAQs, a complete recipe card and separate SEO fields. It has no fixed total word target or mandatory conclusion. The exact ingredient list appears in the recipe card. Empty optional sections do not cause a correction request. General culinary explanations are explicitly distinguished from source evidence; quantities and storage durations still require source support. Unresolved editorial notes appear in review details and `editorial-notes.txt`, outside publishable HTML, text and Markdown. Frozen existing jobs retain their previous writing request and format.

The [recipe-photo-editor skill](../.agents/skills/recipe-photo-editor/SKILL.md) requires current-recipe-only photo discovery: include ingredients, preparation, cooking steps and the finished dish; exclude related posts, recommendations, other recipes, advertisements, portraits, logos and app badges. Apply filtering before automatic selection and estimation, without an extra paid analysis call. Chrome capture v2.0.2 checks article boundaries, nested recommendation markers, links to other articles and recipe metadata. It preserves legitimate recipe images and groups responsive/crop variants. This affects new captures; saved imports and approved jobs keep their existing photographs.

Every new item saves profile versions, instructions, writing request, selected originals, hashes, palette and estimate. Final dispatched requests are saved under `research/requests`. Provider receipts are saved under `research/api-receipts` before image conversion or article file processing. ZIP prompts omit inline base64 for readability; original reference bytes remain in local storage.

Photo understanding is one cached GPT-5.5 pass in groups of at most ten selected originals, with explicit high-detail input. It identifies ingredients, labels, vessel, cooking stage and supported article placement. It does not score quality. Images with unclear context wait for a targeted context review. Unmapped images go in a separate gallery.

Photo profile v5 requests a newly composed home-kitchen photograph of the same dish and cooking stage, with an everyday iPhone 17 Pro Max Photo-mode appearance. Food identity stays fixed: ingredients, preparation forms, pasta shape, proportions, portion scale, sauce color/consistency, texture and doneness. Meaningful arrangements such as layers, toppings, pouring actions and not-yet-mixed ingredients remain intact. The new scene changes framing, vessel position, existing prop relationships and a modest camera angle where the supported action stays clear. Plain, unbranded cookware retains the same category, structural material, overall shape, approximate capacity and functional handles, with different design details and finish. A skillet stays a skillet; cooking food is never moved into a serving dish.

Ingredient photos require a visibly new container layout, preserving every group and moving its paper label with it; overhead framing can remain when needed for readability. Cooking and served photos must not acquire ingredient bowls, labels, props or unsupported garnish. Recurring cookware, utensils, palette and soft side-window light stay consistent across the recipe. Clear glass and bare metal retain their physical properties. Recoloring, mirroring, cropping or replacing only the collage grid is insufficient. Collages retain every stage and action in chronological order. Understanding profile v3 records food identity separately from observed styling, using the existing schema and cached analysis call. No extra quality-scoring or paid review stage is added.

The workflow still uses each photograph's own reference bytes. New composition is a creative direction, not a copyright-clearance or detection-avoidance guarantee. Prefer owned or appropriately licensed reference photos; independent text-based generation remains available. Review actual results before publication. Existing jobs and retries retain their frozen instructions, palettes, request shapes and outputs, including v4. Only newly prepared items use v5. Offline fixture responses verify plumbing and recovery, not the visual success of the new prompt.

The pre-v5 source checkpoint is local Git tag `backup/before-photo-v5-2026-11-11` (commit `2cf7b732c0b51d5e5dc7bd560be7ec4677643fe8`). A separate source ZIP and verified SQLite backup were saved outside the checkout. To roll back the default photo behavior, restore `photoProfile.ts` and its corresponding skill/docs/tests from that tag after checking for newer edits, then rebuild and restart safely. Do not restore the old database over newer jobs or spending records. Existing saved jobs continue to use their own frozen profiles after either an upgrade or rollback.

## Recovery and compatibility

New batches use local storage policy `lossless-webp-v1`, frozen separately from the paid model and prompt settings. Each generated reference photo becomes one WebP master only when lossless encoding is smaller and every decoded RGBA pixel, dimension, orientation and ICC profile is preserved. Otherwise its valid original format is retained. High-bit-depth inputs are retained unchanged. This never uses lossy WebP, near-lossless mode or resizing for reference outputs. Legacy text-image crops and optional title rendering remain as specified before their final lossless encoding. No extra provider request is involved.

New article HTML and Markdown reference the saved master instead of embedding duplicate image bytes on disk. Standalone HTML downloads and previews embed checked images on demand; ZIPs contain the small HTML and its image files. A PNG download is generated locally from WebP without saving a second master. New WordPress uploads always use WebP: existing WebP bytes are retained, while PNG/JPEG masters are losslessly converted and pixel-checked locally for that upload. Unsupported lossless conversions report an error without uploading another format. Existing confirmed uploads are reused; the first article image remains featured. Native provider responses are still retained for billing/recovery, so the PNG-to-WebP saving is not the same as the saving across the whole storage folder. Existing frozen jobs keep their old outputs and repair behavior. Old files are not bulk-converted.

The new encoder preserved all decoded pixels in 22 existing generated PNGs: 49,267,594 bytes became 33,995,146 bytes in memory (31% smaller). Existing image files were not modified. The restarted local app and worker were healthy; all 10 batches, 10 items, 133 stages, 22 photo records, 78 API-call records and 166 artifacts remained unchanged, with all artifact checksums verified. No paid calls or customer WordPress writes were made. A source ZIP and consistent SQLite backup were saved under the owner's Documents backup folder before updating the running app.

Migration 4 uses SQLite's backup API before rebuilding the content-item table to allow imports independently of historical recipe records. Foreign keys are checked before commit. Old batch settings and their three-image stages remain intact. Imports never become verified publications and never create tracked websites.

Disk/export failures reuse saved receipts. An unknown paid outcome keeps its reservation and is not repeated automatically. Retrying one image keeps the article and other images. A changed photo selection before approval invalidates the quote; refresh the estimate before approving. Jobs already approved keep their saved selection. Pending items added to an approved batch require approval of the refreshed estimate.

Original photographs are reference inputs and are shown only in the labeled original/edited comparison for review. Article HTML, Markdown, WordPress copy and recipe-card image fields use saved generated/edited outputs only. A missing, failed or unreadable edit is omitted from the article; the original file or source URL is never substituted. The downloadable comparison gallery is a review artifact, separate from the publishable article.

Reference downloads use the same public-address, redirect, robots and per-domain rate checks as discovery. Uploads are limited to still JPEG, PNG or WebP, 10 MB and 40 megapixels each. Paths and checksums are checked before reading. Authenticated local API routes retain the existing loopback and CSRF protection. Hosted builds open no SQLite database.

Discovery is bounded to 80 candidates per recipe and uses identifiable article containers and metadata. Unusual page layouts, inaccessible CDN images, ambiguous ingredient names and visually different near-duplicates may need manual selection or uploads. Responsive variants and byte-identical or decoded-pixel-identical copies are grouped; unrelated visual similarity is not guessed. ZIP downloads are limited to 250 MB; larger jobs retain individual downloads.

## Chrome capture when a website blocks URL imports

Load the unpacked extension in `extensions/recipe-intelligence-capture` using Chrome's Extensions page. Open the local workspace with its desktop shortcut in the same Chrome profile, then open the recipe website and click **Recipe Intelligence Capture**. Review the article/photo preview and click **Import and see estimate**. The app opens the corresponding unpaid draft; generation still requires the normal Generate button.

The extension transfers sanitized article content and actual photo bytes from Chrome. Browser imports use a separate `capturing` state and never enter the server's URL-fetch queue. Recovery reuses a capture UUID; image checksums deduplicate retry uploads. The import becomes ready only after the extension finishes. Article facts and readable text are saved under `research/browser/`; photos use the existing `research/references/` storage and validation. There are no new schema changes, browser token exports or relaxed origin/CORS checks. See the [extension guide](../extensions/recipe-intelligence-capture/README.md) for installation, recovery and limitations.
