# Vintage Farmhouse complete creative prompt

```text
Create ONE exceptionally beautiful, professional vintage American farmhouse food-blog recipe graphic, art-directed for Facebook in a 4:5 vertical format.

EXACT RECIPE TITLE: "{{TITLE}}"

THE CREATIVE GOAL:
Transform the presentation of my ORIGINAL uploaded food photograph into a premium vintage farmhouse recipe-card design. This should feel like a cherished old family recipe brought to life through expert handcrafted typography, warm color harmony, beautiful ornamental framing, and genuine printed-paper texture. It must NOT look like a generic template or flat image with a rectangle below it.

ORIGINAL PHOTOGRAPH — MOST IMPORTANT:
Use the uploaded original food photograph as the authentic main visual. The original dish's ingredients, food arrangement, surface textures, lighting, color, serving dish and natural imperfections remain unchanged. Do not regenerate, repaint, replace, relight or artificially beautify the meal. Do not invent garnishes, toppings or additional food. Treat the original photo as a protected layer and build the design outside of it.

ART DIRECTION:
Create a charming American country-kitchen aesthetic influenced by nostalgic 1950s home recipe boxes, vintage family cookbook covers, decorative printed recipe labels and hand-finished farmhouse crafts. Make it visually rich, nostalgic and warm but not overly cluttered. Compose the design as if each graphic element had been thoughtfully placed by a talented food-blog designer.

COLOR STORY:
Choose warm aged-ivory paper, buttery cream, muted country red, moss/olive green, warm tobacco brown and a few antique-gold highlights. Adjust the accent hues based on the actual food image so that the meal remains dominant. Natural paper tones and soft shadows make the graphic tactile. Do not recolor the food.

LAYOUT:
The true food photo must dominate the composition, typically taking the upper two-thirds to three-quarters when source proportions allow. Build a delicately framed photographic zone with an organic vintage paper border OUTSIDE the photo. Use a characteristic farmhouse title panel in a dedicated lower area, or another intelligently chosen position that avoids covering food. Make the title plaque feel bespoke: softly scalloped or ornamented corners, subtle cream paper embossing, antique red stitching/line detail, carefully balanced proportions and a realistic printed edge. Use actual visual depth, not a plain beige box. The photo area and label must work together as one cohesive object.

DECORATIVE CHARACTER:
Introduce a restrained but recognizable country-recipe language: tiny daisies or botanical floral sprays at selected corners, muted olive leaves, fine hand-painted border accents, a tasteful red-and-cream gingham strip or bow, small heirloom recipe-card separators, or a tiny red heart if compositionally appropriate. Choose a curated subset; do not scatter all elements everywhere. Decorations belong outside the protected photo. Keep the food large and unobscured.

EXCEPTIONAL TYPOGRAPHY:
Display only the exact title "{{TITLE}}". Use expertly styled vintage typography, not ordinary interface text. Favor a bold classic display serif with rich character, combined with a restrained handwritten or old-fashioned supporting style when needed. Break the title intelligently across lines to emphasize the most appealing words while preserving the full exact wording, punctuation and case. Use expressive sizes and proper text spacing. Warm dark-burgundy/deep brown text on a pale ivory title panel is desirable. Ensure the full title is crisp and readable at mobile size.

HANDCRAFTED FINISH:
Use subtle deckled paper grain, realistic print imperfections, soft natural layered shadows and delicate antique border details. The finished composition should resemble a high-quality printed family recipe keepsake, not fake vector clip art and not a glossy AI food photograph.

STRICTLY AVOID:
Generic white cards, flat photo+frame+title arrangements, large empty dead zones, modern magazine-cover typography, neon gradients, harsh geometric advertising, unrelated food props, extra ingredients, logos, watermarks, recipes, instructions, ratings, multiple photos or collages.

FINAL CHECK:
Produce exactly ONE richly designed, tasteful farmhouse-style food-blog graphic. The real original food photo is the hero. The character must be immediately identifiable as Vintage Farmhouse through its decorated paper architecture, feminine but tasteful flowers/gingham, and professional expressive title lettering.

Generation composition: vertical 4:5. Final intended export: exactly 1200 × 1500 PNG.
```

## Direct image-edit rules

```text
DIRECT IMAGE EDIT RULES
- Produce ONE independent recipe graphic per request, not a collage or multi-panel variations.
- Treat the uploaded original food photograph as the authoritative photographic source.
- Do not invent or replace the food, garnish, utensils, dishware, textures or background within the source photograph.
- In the direct image-edit workflow, send the owner's food photograph as image 1. The image model creates the complete finished design; no later compositor adds the food or title.
- Keep the meal visually dominant. Place type and ornaments on a designed label or frame without hiding important food.
- Use the title string exactly as supplied: {{TITLE}}. If NO TEXT is selected, render no text.
- No invented ingredients, preparation times, ratings, health claims, logos, URLs, or slogans.
- Style variation must be material in hierarchy, typography, graphic treatment and geometry, not only colors.
- The app exports one 1200 × 1500 PNG. Keep the food and complete title within a centered 4:5 safe area because the selected model's native portrait size may differ.
- Direct image edits cannot verify identical source pixels. Compare the finished result with the original photo before publishing.
- Honor user-selected model/quality, budget checks and explicit paid-call consent.
```
